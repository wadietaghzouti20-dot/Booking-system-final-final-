02 — Behind the screen

What happens when you
open a website?

01

You request

You type a website address or tap a link.

02

It finds

DNS helps translate the domain name into an IP address.

03

It travels

Your request and the response move as data across networks.

04

It appears

Your browser receives files and displays the page.

Think of it like a delivery: an address, a route, a package split into packets, then reassembled at the destination.

IP = Internet Protocol · DNS = Domain Name System

04 — Smarter search

Better questions.
Better results.

Search engines match your words with information they have indexed. Specific keywords help narrow the results.

Too broad

“history”

Millions of possible meanings. You still have to do the filtering.

More focused

“causes of the French Revolution 1789”

Clear topic, clear context, more useful starting points.

Use key phrasesAdd dates or placesTry quotation marksCompare results
Search results are starting points — not automatic proof.

05 — Critical thinking

Pause before you
believe or share.

A professional-looking page can still be wrong. Check the source, evidence and context.

✓

Signs of stronger information

Named author or organization · recent date · links to evidence · agreement with other reliable sources · clear corrections policy.

!

Warning signs

Shocking headline · no author · no evidence · urgent pressure to share · copied images or claims with no context.

Quick test: Who says this? How do they know? Can another trustworthy source confirm it?

A secure connection (HTTPS) protects the connection; it does not prove a page is truthful.

08 — Balance

Social media: a tool
or a trap?

Opportunity

What it can do

Help people find communities, share creative work, learn from others and spread useful information.

Risk

What to watch

Endless scrolling, comparison, misinformation, pressure to respond and loss of focus.

Choose when to use it. Don't let it choose for you.

Turn off unnecessary notifications and set intentional time limits.
09 / 16
09 — Safety essentials

Lock the doors to
your digital life.

🔐

Use strong, unique passwords

A long passphrase is easier to remember and harder to guess. Never reuse the same password everywhere.

🛡️

Turn on two-step verification

A second check can protect an account even if a password is stolen.

🎣

Spot phishing

Be careful with urgent messages, unexpected attachments and links asking for passwords or codes.

⚙️

Review privacy settings

Share less personal information and check who can see your posts and profile.

Never share a verification code — even with someone claiming to be support.

11 — Recognize the risks

The online world has
real consequences.

🚫

Cyberbullying

Save evidence, block and report abusive accounts, and speak to a trusted adult.

🧾

Scams

Question prizes, suspicious payments and offers that seem too good to be true.

🧍

Unsafe contact

Protect personal details. Don't meet an online stranger alone; ask a trusted adult for help.

Remember: If something feels wrong, stop replying, keep evidence and get help from someone you trust.

You do not have to handle online harassment or threats alone.
